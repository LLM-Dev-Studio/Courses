# The Frame Phase

Frame agrees what "better" means and where the Initiative is bounded. It has
two fields.

## 4. Define Outcomes

Record desired outcomes and success criteria. Ask questions such as:

> What would be observably different if the problem were solved?

> How would you decide whether the change was successful?

## 5. Set Boundaries

Record non-goals, constraints, assumptions, open questions, and risks. Ask
questions such as:

> What is explicitly outside the scope of this Initiative?

> What constraints must any intervention respect?

> What are we assuming but have not yet proved?

> What still needs an answer before selecting or designing a change?

> What could go wrong if we change this process, or if we do nothing?

## Worked Example

Continuing the variation-approval session, Priya selects **Success criteria**
and sends:

> How would you decide whether the change was successful?

Daniel answers:

> At least 90 percent of complete variation requests should reach a decision
> within two working days. No variation work should begin without recorded
> authority, except under the documented emergency rule.

Priya then moves to **Constraints** and asks what any intervention must
respect. Daniel answers:

> Supervisors mainly use phones on sites with unreliable reception. The
> existing accounting and estimating systems must remain. We have limited
> capacity for training.

Each accepted answer narrows the field of reasonable responses before Shape
ever compares interventions.

## Key Takeaway

Frame turns a vague sense of "better" into measurable outcomes and explicit
boundaries — both of which Shape will need to compare interventions fairly.
